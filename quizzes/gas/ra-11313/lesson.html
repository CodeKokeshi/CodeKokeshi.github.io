Gender and Society - Lesson 7

RA 11313

The Safe Spaces Act, covering gender-based sexual harassment in public spaces, online spaces, workplaces, and educational or training institutions.

Definition of Terms

Catcalling

Unwanted remarks directed toward a person, commonly in the form of wolf-whistling and misogynistic, transphobic, homophobic, and sexist slurs.

Gender

Socially ascribed characteristics, norms, roles, attitudes, values, and expectations identifying social behavior and relations between men and women.

Gender-based online sexual harassment

Online conduct targeted at a person that causes or is likely to cause mental, emotional, or psychological distress and fear of personal safety.

Gender identity and/or expression

A personal sense of identity characterized by clothing, inclinations, and behavior in relation to masculine or feminine conventions.

Public spaces

Streets, alleys, parks, schools, buildings, malls, transportation terminals, markets, government offices, PUVs, app-based vehicles, theaters, spas, and similar places.

Stalking

Repeated visual or physical proximity, non-consensual communication, or a combination thereof that causes fear for safety or emotional distress.

Streets and Public Spaces Sexual Harassment

Gender-based streets and public spaces sexual harassment is committed through unwanted and uninvited sexual actions or remarks against any person, regardless of the motive for committing the action or remarks.

It includes catcalling, wolf-whistling, unwanted invitations, misogynistic, transphobic, homophobic and sexist slurs, persistent uninvited comments or gestures on appearance, relentless requests for personal details, sexual comments and suggestions, public masturbation, flashing, groping, and unwanted verbal or physical advances that threaten personal space and physical safety.

Places Open to the Public and PUVs

Privately-owned public places

Restaurants, bars, cinemas, malls, buildings, resorts, hotels, casinos, and other places open to the public must adopt a zero-tolerance policy.

Victim assistance

Establishments must coordinate with police, make CCTV footage available when ordered by court, and provide a safe gender-sensitive environment for reporting.

Warning signs and officers

Places of recreation must install warning signs and designate at least one anti-sexual harassment officer.

Public utility vehicles

The LTO may cancel licenses and the LTFRB may suspend or revoke franchises when acts are committed in PUVs.

Unlawful Acts in Public Spaces

  • Cursing, wolf-whistling, catcalling, leering, intrusive gazing, taunting, unwanted invitations, slurs, persistent unwanted comments, relentless requests for personal details, sexual jokes, sexual names, comments, and demands.
  • Offensive body gestures, flashing of private parts, public masturbation, groping, and similar lewd sexual actions.
  • Stalking, touching, pinching, brushing against the body, or touching intimate body parts.

Online Sexual Harassment

Gender-based online sexual harassment includes acts using information and communications technology to terrorize or intimidate victims through threats; unwanted sexual, misogynistic, transphobic, homophobic, and sexist remarks; cyberstalking; incessant messaging; sharing sexual media without consent; unauthorized recording and sharing; impersonation; posting lies; and false abuse reports to silence victims.

Qualified Cases

  • The act happens in a common carrier or PUV where the perpetrator is the driver and the offended party is a passenger.
  • The offended party is a minor, senior citizen, PWD, or breastfeeding mother nursing her child.
  • The offended party is diagnosed with a mental problem tending to impair consent.
  • The perpetrator is a uniformed services member and acts while in uniform.
  • The act takes place in a government agency offering frontline services and the perpetrator is a government employee.

Workplace and Educational Institutions

Workplace harassment includes unwelcome sexual advances, requests or demands for sexual favors, conduct of a sexual nature, conduct based on sex affecting dignity, and pervasive conduct creating an intimidating, hostile, or humiliating environment.

Schools must designate an officer-in-charge, provide a gender-sensitive environment, publish grievance procedures, investigate possible acts, take immediate action against hostile environments, and may strip a diploma or issue expulsion when a perpetrator is found guilty.
