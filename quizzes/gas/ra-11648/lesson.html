Gender and Society - Lesson 9

RA 11648

Amendments strengthening protection against rape, sexual exploitation, and abuse, including increasing the age for determining statutory rape.

Purpose of the Act

RA 11648 promotes stronger protection against rape and sexual exploitation and abuse. It increases the age for determining statutory rape and amends the Revised Penal Code, RA 8353, and RA 7610.

Amendment to Article 266-A

Rape is committed by a person who has carnal knowledge of another person under the listed circumstances. The amended provision includes when the offended party is under sixteen years of age or demented, even if none of the other circumstances are present.

Close-in-age exception

There is no criminal liability when the age difference is not more than three years and the sexual act is proven consensual, non-abusive, and non-exploitative. This exception does not apply if the victim is under thirteen years of age.

Non-Abusive and Non-Exploitative

Non-abusive

Absence of undue influence, intimidation, fraudulent machinations, coercion, threat, and physical, sexual, psychological, or mental injury or maltreatment.

Non-exploitative

No actual or attempted act of unfairly taking advantage of the child's vulnerability, differential power, or trust.

Qualified and Simple Seduction

Qualified seduction involves a minor sixteen and over but under eighteen years of age, committed by a person in public authority, priest, home-servant, domestic, guardian, teacher, or any person entrusted with the education or custody of the minor seduced.

Simple seduction involves a minor sixteen and over but under eighteen years of age, committed by means of deceit.

Amendments to Child Protection Provisions

  • Children, whether male or female, who for money, profit, other consideration, coercion, or adult influence engage in sexual intercourse or lascivious conduct are deemed exploited in prostitution and other sexual abuse.
  • When the victim is under sixteen, perpetrators are prosecuted under rape or lascivious conduct provisions, as applicable.
  • Child trafficking includes trading and dealing with children, including buying and selling a child for money, consideration, or barter.
  • The maximum penalty applies to child trafficking when the victim is under sixteen years old.
  • Obscene publications and indecent shows include hiring, employing, using, persuading, inducing, or coercing a child to perform in obscene exhibitions or indecent shows.

Other Acts Prejudicial to Development

A person who keeps or has in company a minor sixteen years of age or under, or who is ten years or more his junior, in places such as hotels, motels, beer joints, discotheques, cabarets, pension houses, saunas, massage parlors, beaches, or tourist places shall suffer the penalty stated in the Act, unless the exception for relation or lawful social, moral, or legal duty applies.
