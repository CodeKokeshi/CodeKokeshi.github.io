Gender and Society - Lesson 3

Gender and Social Construct

This lesson introduces social construction, sociological theories of gender, and gender as a social construction through roles and stereotypes.

Topics

Social construction

How people construct understandings of the world and give meaning to encounters, products, and experiences.

Sociological theories of gender

Structural functionalism, symbolic interactionism, and conflict theory.

Gender as a social construction

Gender roles and gender stereotyping shape ideas of masculinity and femininity.

Social Construction

Social construction

Social construction assumes that people construct, create, make, or invent their understandings of the world and the meanings they give to encounters with others, or various products they or others create.

Sociological Theories of Gender

Structural functionalism

Functionalists argue that gender roles were established well before the pre-industrial era, when men typically took care of responsibilities outside the home, such as hunting, and women typically took care of domestic responsibilities in or around the home.

Symbolic interactionism

Symbolic interactionism aims to understand human behavior by analyzing the critical role of symbols in human interaction.

Conflict theory

Conflict theory sees society as a struggle for dominance among social groups that compete for scarce resources. Social problems are created when dominant groups exploit or oppress subordinate groups.

Gender as a Social Construction

Gender roles

The set of social and behavioral norms that are considered to be socially appropriate for individuals of a specific sex.

Gender stereotypes

Gender stereotypes evolve based on a culture's belief systems regarding the attitudes, behaviors, and other characteristics that seem to differentiate the two sexes.

Key Points

  • Ideas about what it means to be male are connected to a hegemonic view of masculinity.
  • Ideas about what it means to be female are connected to the conceptualization of femininity.
  • The lesson closes with the idea that gender difference is brought about by misconceptions of the nature of male and female.
