Gender and Society - Lesson 4

Gender and Family

The family is the primary social unit and the first environment where gender is learned, practiced, and reinforced.

Understanding Gender in the Family Context

Gender

Gender refers to the socially constructed roles, behaviors, and expectations that societies assign to individuals based on their perceived sex.

Gender socialization

From early childhood, family members teach children what is considered appropriate for boys and girls, shaping future behavior, attitudes, and sense of identity.

Example

Boys are often encouraged to be assertive, independent, and active.

Example

Girls are often expected to be nurturing, obedient, and modest.

Modeling roles

Parents model who earns income, manages the household, disciplines, and cares for children.

Gender Roles Within the Family

Families are traditionally structured around distinct gender roles, often based on cultural norms rather than biological necessity.

Traditional Male RoleTraditional Female Role
Breadwinner, protector, decision-maker.Caregiver, homemaker, nurturer.

These roles can reinforce gender inequality by limiting opportunities and perpetuating stereotypes. Modern families increasingly challenge these roles as women join the workforce and men engage in caregiving.

Gender Socialization and Its Impacts

Observation

Children imitate the behaviors of parents and siblings.

Instruction

Parents reward or discourage behaviors depending on gender norms.

Division of labor

Chores and expectations often differ by gender.

Media influence

Family-approved media reinforces gender stereotypes, such as princesses for girls and heroes for boys.

This early gender learning influences personality, career aspirations, relationships, and perceptions of equality in adulthood.

Gender Inequality in Families

Despite social progress, gender inequality often persists within families worldwide due to unequal access to resources, decision-making, and opportunities.

  • Women doing the majority of unpaid care and housework.
  • Men having greater authority over finances and decisions.
  • Women facing domestic or intimate partner violence.
  • Girls receiving less education or opportunities in some cultures.

Changing Family Dynamics

Dual-income families

Financial responsibilities are shared.

Single-parent households

They often challenge gender norms, such as single fathers as nurturers.

Non-traditional families

Same-sex couples and non-traditional families highlight that love and responsibility are not determined by gender.

Legal frameworks

Paternity leave, shared custody, and domestic partnership rights support more flexible family roles.

Gender and Family in the Filipino Setting

The Philippine Constitution recognizes the family as the foundation of the nation and mandates the State to strengthen family solidarity. Filipino families are characterized by strong emotional ties, respect for elders, close kinship networks, and deep religious influence.

Traditional roles

Fathers are expected to be providers and heads of the family; mothers are seen as primary caregivers responsible for raising children and managing the home.

Children

Children are socialized to respect authority and fulfill gender-based duties, such as sons helping with outdoor work and daughters assisting in household chores.

Roots

These roles have been shaped by colonial history, Spanish patriarchy, Catholic teachings, and traditions emphasizing male authority and female modesty.

Evolving Gender Roles in Modern Filipino Families

  • Globalization, education, and migration have reshaped Filipino gender dynamics.
  • Women’s workforce participation has increased, and many Filipino women now hold professional and leadership roles.
  • Men are becoming more involved in domestic responsibilities, especially in urban areas and OFW households.
  • Many Filipino mothers now work abroad while fathers take on caregiving roles, reversing traditional expectations.

Gender Issues and Efforts Toward Equality

Unequal division of labor

Women still perform most household and caregiving tasks, even when employed.

Domestic violence

Gender-based violence remains a serious issue.

Decision-making power

In some families, men still dominate major financial and household decisions.

Health rights

Women may have limited autonomy in reproductive decisions due to taboos and religious influence.

Stereotyping

Boys are discouraged from expressing emotions, while girls face expectations of modesty, domesticity, and sacrifice.

Efforts include RA 9710, RA 9262, RA 8187, GAD education, shared parenting, equal decision-making, LGU GAD programs, and family programs emphasizing partnership and mutual respect.

Conclusion

The relationship between gender and family is dynamic and evolving. Families serve as the first school of gender, where roles and expectations are learned. In the Philippine setting, traditional values of male leadership and female caregiving persist but are being challenged by modern realities, education, and advocacy for equality.

Achieving gender equality within Filipino families is key to building a fair, inclusive, and progressive society that values partnership over patriarchy and respect over hierarchy.
