Gender and Society - Lesson 2

Understanding SOGIESC

SOGIESC helps us talk about sexual orientation, gender identity, gender expression, and sex characteristics without reducing people to assumptions or stereotypes.

Why SOGIESC Matters

Gender issues may involve prejudice, violence, abuse, harassment, and discrimination. Internally, people may also face stigmatization, health-care concerns, substance use, mental health issues, and pressure from the cultural environment.

These experiences can be connected with sadness, depression, anxiety, anger, disgust, confusion, and dissatisfaction. Discussing SOGIESC gives us language for understanding these concerns more clearly.

Sexual Orientation

Sexual orientation

Each person's capacity for profound emotional, affectional, and sexual attraction to, and intimate and sexual relations with, others. It answers: "To whom am I attracted? Who do I love?"

Romantic orientation

Who you are romantically attracted to, meaning who you want to be in a romantic relationship with. It is unrelated to sexual attraction.

Sexual orientation

Who you are sexually attracted to, meaning who you get turned on by or who you would want to engage in sexual behaviors with.

Common Sexual Orientations

Heterosexual

Attracted to a person of a different or opposite gender identity.

Homosexual

Attracted to a person of the same gender identity.

Bisexual

Attracted to persons of two or more genders, or to a person of any two or more genders.

Pansexual

Attracted to people of all genders and/or does not concern gender when attracted toward someone.

Androsexual

A person attracted to masculine gender presentation.

Gynosexual

A person attracted to feminine gender presentation.

Asexual

A person who does not have sexual attraction to anyone.

Gender Identity

Gender identity

Each person's deeply felt internal and individual experience of gender, which may or may not correspond with the sex assigned at birth. It answers: "Who am I?"

Cisgender

A person whose gender identity and biological sex assigned at birth matched.

Gender fluid

A person who may always feel like a mix of the two traditional genders: man and woman.

Transgender

A person whose gender identity does not align with, or is different from, the sex assigned at birth.

Agender

A person without gender identity or who does not identify with a particular gender.

Gender Expression and Sex Characteristics

Gender expression

The way a person expresses gender identity, typically through appearance, dress, and behavior.

Sex characteristics

Biological physical traits that indicate biological sex, such as chromosomes, genitalia, gonads, hormones, and reproductive organs.

The lesson identifies sex characteristics with male, female, and intersex categories.

In a Nutshell

  • We live in a world of diversity.
  • SOGIESC should not be used for unequal treatment and discrimination.
  • SOGIESC should be discussed.
  • We invest on our differences.
  • We are duty-bearers mandated to protect and promote the rights of everyone.
  • We cannot force anyone to disclose SOGIESC or assume it for them.

Building Resilience

Face the reality

Recognize the existence of prejudice and discrimination instead of minimizing them.

Make meaning

Understand experiences in a way that helps people move forward.

Control what you can

Focus energy on actions and responses within one's control.

Advocate for change

Work toward safer, fairer, and more inclusive spaces.
