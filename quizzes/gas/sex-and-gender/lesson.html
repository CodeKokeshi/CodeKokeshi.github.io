Gender and Society - Lesson 1

Sex and Gender

As you explore various aspects of gender and development, it is necessary to obtain a clearer understanding of what sex and gender are by delving into their respective definitions and constructs.

Definition of Sex

Sex

Sex refers to the biological and physiological characteristics that define humans as male, female, or intersex.

Biological characteristics

These characteristics are innate and universal, determined by anatomy, chromosomes, and hormones.

Chromosomes

Females typically have XX chromosomes; males have XY.

Reproductive organs

Ovaries and uterus in females; testes and penis in males.

Secondary sex characteristics

Body hair, breast development, voice pitch, and related traits.

Key idea

Sex is biological, generally assigned at birth, and rarely changes, though some individuals may undergo medical transitions to align their physical characteristics with their gender identity.

Definition of Gender

Gender

Gender refers to the socially constructed roles, behaviors, expectations, and attributes that a society considers appropriate for men, women, and people of other gender identities.

How gender is shaped

It is shaped by culture, history, religion, and social norms, and can vary across time and place.

Gender role example

Expecting women to take care of the home and children.

Gender role example

Expecting men to be the breadwinners.

Gender role example

Assuming only men can hold leadership or political roles.

Key idea

Gender is not biological. It is socially and culturally defined and can change over time as society evolves.

Sex vs. Gender: The Difference

Aspect Sex Gender
Basis Biological: chromosomes, anatomy, hormones. Social and cultural norms and expectations.
Nature Innate and generally fixed. Learned, dynamic, and may change.
Examples Male, female, intersex. Masculine, feminine, non-binary, transgender.
Determined by Biology. Society and culture.
Focuses on Physical traits. Roles, behaviors, identity.

Why It Is Important to Distinguish Between Sex and Gender

Understanding the difference helps in promoting gender equality and addressing gender-based discrimination.

Disaster response

Recognizing gender differences helps ensure that both men's and women's needs are addressed, such as access to reproductive health care or stigma around seeking emotional help.

Education

Understanding gender roles prevents stereotyping, such as girls being discouraged from STEM fields.

Workplaces

Gender awareness promotes equal opportunities and inclusive environments.

The Broader Spectrum of Gender

Modern discussions recognize that gender is not binary, meaning not only male or female.

Transgender

Gender identity differs from sex assigned at birth.

Non-binary

Does not fit within the traditional male/female categories.

Gender-fluid

Gender identity may change over time or context.

Recognizing this diversity fosters inclusivity and respect for human rights.

Summary

Sex = biological and physical characteristics.
Gender = social and cultural roles, expectations, and identities.
Both interact to shape people's experiences, opportunities, and treatment in society.
Understanding both concepts is key to achieving gender equality and ensuring that everyone, regardless of sex or gender, enjoys equal rights and opportunities.
