RA 8353

The Anti-Rape Law, classifying rape as a crime against persons and defining when and how rape is committed.

Rape as a Crime Against Persons

RA 8353 classifies the crime of rape as a crime against persons under the Revised Penal Code. It incorporates a chapter on rape into Title Eight of the Code.

Article 266-A: When and How Committed

Carnal knowledge

Rape is committed by a man who has carnal knowledge of a woman through force, threat, or intimidation; when the offended party is deprived of reason or unconscious; by fraudulent machination or grave abuse of authority; or when the offended party is under twelve years of age or demented.

Sexual assault

Rape is also committed by any person who, under the same circumstances, commits sexual assault by inserting a penis into another person's mouth or anal orifice, or any instrument or object into the genital or anal orifice of another person.

Penalties and Aggravating Circumstances

Rape under paragraph 1 is punished by reclusion perpetua. The penalty increases when committed with a deadly weapon or by two or more persons, when the victim becomes insane, when attempted rape is connected with homicide, or when homicide is committed by reason or on the occasion of rape.

  • The victim is under eighteen and the offender is a parent, ascendant, step-parent, guardian, relative within the third civil degree, or common-law spouse of the parent.
  • The victim is under police, military, law enforcement, or penal custody.
  • The rape is committed in full view of the spouse, parent, children, or relatives within the third civil degree.
  • The victim is a religious engaged in legitimate religious vocation and personally known as such by the offender.
  • The victim is a child below seven years old.
  • The offender knows they have HIV/AIDS or another sexually transmissible disease and transmits it to the victim.
  • The offender is a member of the AFP, PNP, law enforcement, or penal institution and uses the position to facilitate the crime.
  • The victim suffers permanent physical mutilation or disability.
  • The offender knew of the offended party's pregnancy.
  • The offender knew of the offended party's mental disability, emotional disorder, or physical handicap.

Paragraph 2 Penalties

Rape under paragraph 2 is punished by prision mayor. Penalties increase when committed with a deadly weapon or by two or more persons, when the victim becomes insane, when attempted rape and homicide occur, when homicide occurs by reason or on the occasion of rape, or when any of the ten aggravating or qualifying circumstances are present.

Effect of Pardon and Presumptions

The subsequent valid marriage between the offended party and offender extinguishes the criminal action or penalty imposed. If the legal husband is the offender, forgiveness by the wife extinguishes the criminal action or penalty, unless the marriage is void ab initio.

Any physical overt act manifesting resistance, or a situation rendering the offended party incapable of giving valid consent, may be accepted as evidence in prosecution.
